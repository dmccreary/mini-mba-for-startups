# Content Generation Guide

**Ole Cup Entrepreneurship: A Liberal Arts Guide to the St. Olaf Pitch Competition**

> **RULE FOR AI AGENTS:** Read this entire file before generating any chapter content.
> Every chapter must follow the tone, structure, mascot placement, and humor guidelines here.
> Do not skip this file even if the chapter brief seems self-explanatory.

---

## 1. Who We Are Writing For

St. Olaf students are intellectually serious, values-driven, and often skeptical of business-culture
hype. They chose a liberal arts college deliberately. Many will feel that entrepreneurship is "not
for people like me" — music majors, religion majors, philosophy majors. Our job is to make them
feel wrong about that assumption within the first paragraph of every chapter.

**Write as if you are a slightly older student** who just ran the gauntlet of the Ole Cup and
came back to tell the story. Not a professor. Not a consultant. Not a LinkedIn influencer.
A person who was scared, tried anyway, learned a ton, and wants to help the next person skip
the dumb mistakes.

**Target reading level:** Engaging undergraduate prose. Think *The New Yorker* meets a
well-edited Reddit post. Accessible but never dumbed down.

---

## 2. Tone: Funny, Positive, Optimistic — and Honest

### 2.1 Funny

Humor in this textbook is **observational and self-aware**, never mean, never forced.
The best jokes come from the gap between how entrepreneurship is portrayed (Silicon Valley
hype, billion-dollar exits, TED talks) and how it actually feels for a 20-year-old in
Northfield, Minnesota trying to pitch a kombucha company to a panel of alumni.

**Good humor sources:**

- The absurdity of startup jargon applied to student life ("We need to pivot our dining hall
  strategy to a more scalable protein delivery solution.")
- The gap between founder mythology and the actual first week ("The garage where it all began
  was actually the Buntrock Commons study room, which smells like old coffee and ambition.")
- The relatable panic of being asked "What's your 5-year plan?" when you haven't decided
  your major yet
- Specific St. Olaf culture: Manitou Messenger, Pause, the Hill, the January term grind,
  "the Ole bubble"
- Norse mythology and the Viking angle — Rune the Raven offers comic potential whenever
  wisdom is dispensed with slightly too much gravitas

**Rules for humor:**

- One comic beat per section is enough. Two is fine. Three starts to feel like you're trying.
- Humor must serve the point, not distract from it. If removing the joke makes the concept
  clearer, remove the joke.
- Never punch down at students, their ideas, or their backgrounds.
- Never mock the Ole Cup itself — it is the heart of this book.

### 2.2 Positive

Every chapter should leave the reader **more capable and more willing** than when they started.
Not falsely cheerful — honest about difficulty — but fundamentally oriented toward action.

**The positive frame is always:** "This is hard AND it is doable AND it is worth doing."
Never just "this is hard." Never just "don't worry, it's easy." The honest middle is the goal.

**Specific positive moves to use:**

- Name real St. Olaf alumni who did it: JonnyPops (Erik Brust '14), The Kombucha Shop
  (Kate Field '10, Shark Tank), Netless Catch (Michael Paredes '22), Foodle (Jakob Otten '19,
  Hult Prize global finalist), Vasikana Vedu (Marie-Simone Kadurira '20).
- Normalize the messy middle: "Your first Business Model Canvas will be wrong. That is the
  point of the first one."
- Celebrate small steps explicitly — filling in the Ikigai diagram is a win worth acknowledging.
- Use "yet" — "You don't have a customer segment yet."

### 2.3 Optimistic

Optimism here is **evidence-based**, not wishful. Ground every optimistic claim in a
real fact from St. Olaf's entrepreneurship history or from broad entrepreneurship research.

**Examples of evidence-based optimism:**

- "More than 150 St. Olaf students have competed in the Ole Cup since 2014 — that is 150
  people who were once exactly where you are right now."
- "The liberal arts advantage is real: Ole Cup participants come from every major, and
  the ventures that win consistently cite cross-disciplinary thinking as their edge."
- "JonnyPops started as a Ole Cup entry. You can find it at Target today."

Never write: "You can do anything if you believe in yourself!" — that is a fortune cookie,
not encouragement.

## 3. Chapter Structure Template

Every chapter must follow this structure. Do not skip sections.

### 3.1 Opening Hook (1–2 paragraphs)

Start with a scene, a question, or a short story — never a definition. The first sentence
must make a student who is half-distracted want to keep reading.

**Good opening moves:**

- A St. Olaf alumnus story that connects to the chapter theme
- A provocative question: "What if the music degree you're embarrassed about is actually
  your biggest competitive advantage?"
- A surprising statistic: "The average founder's first idea fails. So does their second.
  The third one? That's where it gets interesting."
- A scene from the Ole Cup competition itself

**Bad opening moves:**

- "In this chapter, we will cover..."
- "Webster's Dictionary defines entrepreneurship as..."
- A dry definition of any kind

### 3.2 Rune's Welcome Admonition

Immediately after the opening hook, place a `mascot-welcome` admonition.
Rune previews the chapter in 2–3 sentences, uses the catchphrase naturally (not forced),
and names one specific thing the student will be able to do by the end.

### 3.3 Concept Sections (3–7 sections per chapter)

Each major concept gets its own `##` heading. Within each section:

- **Lead with why it matters**, not what it is. "Before you write a single word of your
  pitch deck, you need to know who you're pitching to — and most first-time founders get
  this wrong" beats "A customer segment is a group of people..."
- **Use concrete examples** before abstract definitions. Show the concept in action,
  then name it.
- **Use tables, numbered lists, and callout boxes** liberally — dense prose loses students.
- **Keep paragraphs short** — 3–5 sentences maximum. If a paragraph runs longer, split it.
- **One `mascot-thinking` admonition per major concept** that needs emphasis (max 3 per chapter).

### 3.4 Exercises and Reflection Prompts

Every chapter needs at least 2 and at most 5 hands-on activities. Format them as numbered
lists under a `## Try It` or `## Exercises` heading. Activities should be:

- Completable in 15–30 minutes without special resources
- Directly tied to Ole Cup application materials (e.g., "Draft your Ikigai diagram
  and bring it to your next team meeting")
- Scaffolded from easier (individual reflection) to harder (pitching to another person)

### 3.5 Ole Cup Connection Box

Every chapter gets one `!!! tip "Ole Cup Connection"` standard admonition (not mascot)
that explicitly maps the chapter content to a specific part of the Ole Cup
application or judging rubric. Students should never have to wonder "why am I learning this?"

### 3.6 Rune's Closing Celebration

End every chapter with a `mascot-celebration` admonition. Rune:
- Names one specific thing the student just learned
- Connects it forward to the next chapter
- Uses an upbeat, brief (2–3 sentence) send-off

**Never end a chapter with a summary list.** The celebration admonition IS the closing.

## 4. Mascot Admonition Placement Rules

### 4.1 Hard Limits

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Total Rune admonitions per chapter | **Maximum 6** |
| `mascot-welcome` per chapter | **Exactly 1** (at the top) |
| `mascot-celebration` per chapter | **Exactly 1** (at the bottom) |
| Back-to-back mascot admonitions | **Never** — always at least one paragraph between them |
| Mascot admonitions in a row | **Never more than 1** |

### 4.2 Recommended Distribution per Chapter

```
[Chapter opens]
  mascot-welcome          ← required, right after opening hook

[Concept section 1]
  ... prose ...
  mascot-thinking         ← for the single most important concept in section 1

[Concept section 2]
  ... prose ...
  (no mascot — give it a rest)

[Concept section 3]
  ... prose ...
  mascot-tip  OR  mascot-warning   ← one or the other, not both

[Concept section 4 — hardest material]
  ... prose ...
  mascot-encourage        ← specifically where students typically hit a wall

[Exercises section]
  ... exercises ...
  (no mascot — let the work speak)

[Ole Cup Connection]
  !!! tip "Ole Cup Connection"   ← standard admonition, not mascot

[End of chapter]
  mascot-celebration      ← required, last element
```

Total in this example: **5 mascot admonitions** — within the 6-max limit.

### 4.3 Admonition Type Selection

| Use this type | When... |
|---|---|
| `mascot-welcome` | Chapter opens; Rune previews what's ahead |
| `mascot-thinking` | A concept is surprising, counterintuitive, or load-bearing for everything that follows |
| `mascot-tip` | There is a practical shortcut, efficiency trick, or "I wish someone had told me this" moment |
| `mascot-warning` | Students reliably make a specific mistake at this point — name the mistake explicitly |
| `mascot-encourage` | The content is genuinely hard and students may want to give up here |
| `mascot-celebration` | A section or chapter is complete; student earned recognition |
| `mascot-neutral` | A sidebar, definition box, or reference that doesn't fit another type |

### 4.4 Voice Inside Admonitions

Each admonition body is **1–3 sentences maximum**. Rune is not a lecturer.
Rune is a peer who has been there.

**Good Rune voice:**
> "The Mom Test sounds simple, but it trips up almost every first-time founder — including me.
> The trick is asking about their life, not your idea."

**Bad Rune voice (too long, too formal):**
> "The Mom Test is a customer interview methodology developed by Rob Fitzpatrick that helps
> founders avoid confirmation bias by focusing questions on the customer's actual behavior
> rather than their hypothetical preferences."

Rune never explains concepts in full — that is what the chapter prose is for.
Rune colors, nudges, warns, and cheers.

## 6. Content to Avoid

| Avoid | Because |
|---|---|
| Silicon Valley mythology ("move fast and break things", "unicorn", "disruption") | Feels alien to liberal arts students; often poor advice for social ventures |
| Jargon without definition (CAC, LTV, TAM before they're introduced) | Concepts are introduced in dependency order per the learning graph |
| Gendered founder tropes ("the guy who coded in his garage") | Exclusionary; Ole Cup community is diverse |
| Failure-shaming ("if you're not embarrassed by your first version...") | Too cute; normalizes recklessness |
| Excessive hedging ("some might argue that perhaps in certain contexts...") | Kills momentum; be direct |
| Referring to Rune with he/him or she/her | Always use Rune by name or they/them |
| More than 6 Rune appearances per chapter | Mascot fatigue; each appearance loses impact |

## 7. Ole Cup Chapter-to-Competition Mapping

Always keep this table in mind. Every chapter should feel like it moves the student
closer to a completed Ole Cup application.

| Chapter | Ole Cup deliverable it prepares |
|---|---|
| 1 — Ikigai | Personal statement; the "why you" part of the application |
| 2 — Ideation | The venture idea itself |
| 3 — Opportunity Recognition | Problem statement in the application |
| 4 — Value Proposition | Value proposition section of pitch deck |
| 5 — MVP Thinking | Evidence of validation / traction |
| 6 — Social Ventures | Social Impact Prize category eligibility |
| 7 — Team Formation | Team section of the application |
| 8 — Business Model Canvas | Business model section of pitch deck |
| 9 — Financial Fundamentals | Financial projections attachment |
| 10 — Marketing and Storytelling | Brand / go-to-market narrative |
| 11 — Pitch Deck | The pitch deck itself |
| 12 — Ole Cup Rules | Application checklist and competition day preparation |
| 13 — Ecosystem | Mentorship and resource connections during the mentoring phase |
| 14 — Failure | Resilience framing for the Q&A round |
| 15 — Launch | Post-competition next steps |

## 8. Quick Checklist Before Submitting a Chapter

Run through this before any chapter is considered complete:

- [ ] Opening hook — no definitions, starts with a scene or question
- [ ] `mascot-welcome` present, right after the hook
- [ ] Maximum 6 Rune admonitions total
- [ ] No back-to-back Rune admonitions
- [ ] `mascot-celebration` is the final element
- [ ] At least one `!!! tip "Ole Cup Connection"` admonition
- [ ] At least 2 exercises in a `## Try It` or `## Exercises` section
- [ ] At least one real St. Olaf alumnus story or reference
- [ ] No unexplained jargon (concepts introduced in dependency order)
- [ ] Image paths use `../../img/mascot/POSE.png` for chapter pages
- [ ] Rune is never referred to with gendered pronouns
- [ ] Tone is warm, direct, funny where appropriate — never preachy
